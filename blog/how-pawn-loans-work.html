How pawn loans actually work in South Africa.

You hand over an item, walk out with cash, and get the item back when you repay. Here's the whole system — costs, rights, storage and all — without the jargon.

Pawn loans are one of the oldest forms of credit in the world, and in South Africa they're a lifeline for people who can't or don't want to use a bank. Here's exactly how a pawn loan works, start to finish.

The basic mechanics

A pawn loan is credit secured by a physical item. You bring something of value — gold, a watch, a vehicle — we value it, and we advance you a portion of that value in cash. The item stays with us, in secure storage, until you repay the loan plus the agreed charges. Then you get your item back, exactly as you left it.

No credit bureau checks are run. The item is the security, which is why the transaction is private, fast, and available the same day.

What it costs

Every pawnbroker sets their own charges, and the law requires those charges to be disclosed upfront in writing before you commit — the amount advanced, the period of the loan, and exactly what repayment will cost. If a lender won't put the numbers in writing, walk away. We put everything in writing on every deal, and we're happy to explain any line of it.

Your rights under the law

Pawnbrokers in South Africa operate under the Second-Hand Goods Act (we must be SAPS-registered and keep records of every item) and the National Credit Act framework (loan terms must be documented and fair). Practically, that means:

  • ✓ You get written terms before you commit
  • ✓ Your item is kept insured and secure
  • ✓ You know exactly what happens if you can't repay
  • ✓ No surprise deductions — the agreed numbers are the numbers

The end of the term

When you repay, you walk out with your item. If you can't repay by the deadline, the standard outcome is that the item is sold to settle the loan — read our honest explainer on what happens if you can't repay. The important thing is that this outcome is agreed and written down on day one, not sprung on you at the end.

Pawn or sell?

If you'll never want the item back, selling usually gets you a better number — we explain the trade-offs in pawning vs selling. If the item matters to you and your cash problem is temporary, pawning is built exactly for that.
