Gold prices · 28 Sep 2026

Spot price, karats and ZAR per gram — the honest math.

Why two 10-gram chains get two different offers, what the "spot price" actually is, and exactly how your payout is worked out.

If you've ever compared offers from two gold buyers and wondered why they differ — or why a buyer can't just "tell you a price" over the phone — this guide is for you. Gold pricing isn't complicated, but it has moving parts. Here they all are, in order.

1. The spot price: gold's global market value

Gold trades on international markets 24 hours a day, and its price — the spot price — moves constantly. It's quoted in US dollars per troy ounce (about 31.1 grams). Because the rand moves against the dollar every day too, the rand value of gold changes even on days when the dollar price doesn't. This is why any buyer quoting you a "fixed per-gram rate forever" is being misleading: the honest number is always linked to the day's spot price and the USD/ZAR exchange rate.

2. Karat: how much of your item is actually gold

Pure gold is 24 karat, but pure gold is soft — most jewellery is alloyed with other metals for strength. The karat stamp tells you the fraction that is real gold:

  • ✓ 9ct (375) — 37.5% pure gold
  • ✓ 14ct (585) — 58.5% pure gold
  • ✓ 18ct (750) — 75.0% pure gold
  • ✓ 22ct (916) — 91.6% pure gold
  • ✓ 24ct (999) — 99.9% pure gold

So a 10-gram 18ct chain contains 7.5 grams of actual gold — and that 7.5 grams is what the spot price applies to, not the full 10.

3. The payout formula

Put it together and the arithmetic is:

gram rate for your karat = spot value (24ct) × purity fraction
your estimate = gram rate × weight in grams

A dealer then applies a small margin between the market value and what they pay — that margin is how any buyer covers testing, refining costs and overhead. It's why our offers are shown as a range, and why comparing two buyers' final numbers (not their margins) is the only comparison that matters.

4. Try it yourself

Our gold calculator does this math for you — pick your karat, enter the weight, and you'll see an indicative range based on the day's rates. The final figure is always confirmed in store after testing, because until purity is verified, any number is a guess.
