How we test your gold before we pay.

No mystery, no guesswork. Here is exactly what happens to your jewellery on our counter — and why testing in front of you is the only way to be paid fairly.

One of the most common worries people have about selling gold in Johannesburg is simple: how do I know they're testing it properly? Fair question. Here is our process, step by step, so you know what to expect before you walk in.

Step 1: Hallmarks and visual inspection

We start with the small stamps most people never notice. A "375" means 9ct gold, "585" means 14ct, "750" means 18ct, and "916" means 22ct. Rings, chains and bangles from reputable jewellers carry these marks, and they give us a strong first indication of purity. Fake stamps exist, which is exactly why a hallmark is only the beginning — never the whole answer.

Step 2: The magnet test

Gold is not magnetic. If a "gold" chain jumps toward our magnet, it contains iron or nickel — usually gold-plated steel or another base metal. This takes two seconds and instantly rules out the most common counterfeits. It doesn't prove an item is gold, but it proves many items are not.

Step 3: Acid testing

For items without a hallmark — old rings, dental gold, scrap pieces, unmarked chains — we use acid testing. A tiny scratch is made on the touchstone, drop of acid is applied, and the reaction tells us the karat. Different strengths of acid correspond to different purities: if the mark survives 18ct acid but fades with 22ct acid, we know what we're holding. The test area is smaller than a pinhead and doesn't damage the piece in any way you'd notice.

Step 4: Weighing — in front of you

Once purity is confirmed, the item goes on a calibrated electronic scale while you watch. The price is then a straightforward calculation: purity fraction × weight × the day's gold value. If you want to see the math, ask — we show it. There is no "back room scale".

What that means for your payout

Because every step happens on the counter, you can follow your own valuation from start to finish. If anything we do seems unclear, ask us to explain it — a buyer who won't explain their testing is a buyer to walk away from.
